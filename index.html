A revision is a second request that bills zero

Per-project billing charges for every pass. A scope change is a change order, a second draft is a second invoice, and the budget watches the attempt count. A flat subscription moves the meter: one number a month, and the flat fee stays flat no matter how many passes the work takes.

The mechanics make the difference countable. Poket Dev runs one active request at a time, quotes a 72-hour turnaround, and allows unlimited revisions until the work is approved. A job that goes through the shop three times is still one request. The queue, the cycles, and the re-opens all sit inside the flat fee.

Price the yearly rung per shipped request: $4,995 a month is $59,940 a year; at a steady 72-hour cadence that is about 120 shipped requests, $499.50 each. That is the per-request price if nothing ever needs a second pass.

Real queues have rework. Say half the requests come back once for changes before approval: the year now holds about 180 build cycles across the same 120 approvals, and the invoice is still $59,940. Cost per build cycle falls to $333 while cost per approved request stays put. Cycle cost fell because cycles were never the thing being priced.

The monthly rung makes the same point. One active job at $5,995 that takes three cycles to approve prices each pass at $1,998.33, and a fourth pass in the same month would cost the buyer nothing extra. A change-order shop cannot hand over pass four at zero; the model does not allow it.

The flat fee reads as a price per approved outcome with the rework pre-paid. Poket Dev prices the outcome; the unlimited revisions clause is the part that would cost extra anywhere else.
